# Lateral Coordination

Coordination through interactions among participants rather than only through directives from a central controller. In [[Dynamics of Complex Systems]], §9.4.4 (printed pp.812–813; PDF pp.828–829), lateral interactions create correlations in activity that were previously imposed by management. Without central control, coordinated behavior still requires interacting participants, external influences and mutual agreement. [source PDF](https://fernandonogueiracosta.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/yaneer-bar-yam-dynamics-of-complex-systems.pdf#page=828)

This complements the qualified [[Hierarchical Control Bottleneck]] argument: removing management alone does not supply a coordination mechanism. On p.813 Bar-Yam distinguishes general coordination networks from markets, whose interactions concerning a resource are summarised through its changing price. [source PDF](https://fernandonogueiracosta.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/yaneer-bar-yam-dynamics-of-complex-systems.pdf#page=829)

**Application to agents:** peer messaging, negotiation and [[Interaction Protocols]] are possible mechanisms for lateral coordination. The book does not supply an agreement algorithm or prove that decentralisation always outperforms central control. [[Self-Organisation]] describes a broader process; not every instance requires explicit messages or agreements.
