# Recipient-Relative Information

The information a message adds depends on the recipient's existing knowledge and model of its source. In [[Dynamics of Complex Systems]], §1.8.5 (printed pp.233–235; PDF pp.249–251), Bar-Yam explains why human communication cannot always be characterised by assuming a completely known source distribution. Repeating an already-known message adds little information; overlapping knowledge between participants can be essential to communicating at all. [source PDF](https://fernandonogueiracosta.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/yaneer-bar-yam-dynamics-of-complex-systems.pdf#page=249)

**Application to agents:** compare the message with the receiving agent's prior context before using message length as a proxy for information transfer. This is a wiki application of the human-communication argument, not an evaluated agent metric.

**Distinction:** shared information is related to [[Common Ground]], but does not establish mutual presupposition or [[Common Knowledge]]. [[Mutual Information]] is a statistical quantity; it does not by itself measure pragmatic understanding.
