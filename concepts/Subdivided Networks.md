# Subdivided Networks

Networks organised into subnetworks with stronger internal coupling and weaker coupling between them. In [[Dynamics of Complex Systems]], §2.4 (printed pp.342–343; PDF pp.358–359), disconnected neural subdivisions allow unconstrained recombination of subpatterns, while partial interconnection constrains which composite patterns remain stable. Intermediate coupling therefore combines reuse with compatibility constraints. [source PDF](https://fernandonogueiracosta.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/yaneer-bar-yam-dynamics-of-complex-systems.pdf#page=358)

Section 2.6 (printed pp.364–365; PDF pp.380–381) extends this to nested subdivisions: a module must retain enough integrity to function while still being influenced by other modules. This structural hierarchy differs from a command hierarchy governed by the [[Hierarchical Control Bottleneck]]. [source PDF](https://fernandonogueiracosta.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/yaneer-bar-yam-dynamics-of-complex-systems.pdf#page=380)

**Application to agents:** modular specialists may benefit from selective cross-module communication that preserves local capabilities and constrains incompatible combinations. This is a neural-model analogy, not a proof of protocol correctness or semantic [[Compositionality]]. The model's approximate limit on useful subdivision must not be read as a universal seven-agent team-size rule.
