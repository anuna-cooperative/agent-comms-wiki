# Usage-Based Grammar

An account in which grammatical knowledge is built from experience with language: speakers categorise encountered utterances into constructions, or form–meaning pairings, ranging from particular expressions to more general patterns. Frequency, context, and co-occurrence influence the organisation and accessibility of those constructions. Lexicon and grammar are intertwined, and adult grammars can continue to change through use.

[[Language Is a Complex Adaptive System - Position Paper]] develops this perspective on pp.5–7 ([source PDF](https://www.unm.edu/~jbybee/downloads/BecknerEtAl2009ComplexAdaptiveSystem.pdf#page=5)) and connects it to acquisition, cultural transmission, and [[Grammaticalization]]. The account concerns human linguistic cognition; treating an artificial agent's learned message patterns in the same way requires an explicit model and evaluation.
