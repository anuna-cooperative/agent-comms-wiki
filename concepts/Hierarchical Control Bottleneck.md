# Hierarchical Control Bottleneck

In [[Dynamics of Complex Systems]], §9.4.3 (printed pp.805–809; PDF pp.821–825), Bar-Yam argues that collective behavior controlled by a single individual cannot exceed the behavioral complexity that individual can supply. **The assumption matters:** p.808 treats lateral communication as nonessential to producing the correlations that constitute collective behavior. The bound concerns the centrally controlled behavior, not all behavior in every organisation with a hierarchy. [source PDF](https://fernandonogueiracosta.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/yaneer-bar-yam-dynamics-of-complex-systems.pdf#page=824)

Section 9.4.4 (printed pp.810–813; PDF pp.826–829) describes management as filtering information upward and elaborating commands downward. That organisation works while the required behavior can be meaningfully simplified for the controller. [[Lateral Coordination]] becomes necessary in the proposed transition when those simplifications cease to suffice. [source PDF](https://fernandonogueiracosta.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/yaneer-bar-yam-dynamics-of-complex-systems.pdf#page=828)

**Application to agents:** examine whether an orchestrator's summaries and decision process retain the distinctions needed to coordinate the task. An agent team with meaningful peer interactions need not satisfy the model's assumption. The book establishes neither a universal failure of orchestration nor an empirical latency bound for software agents. A structural hierarchy of [[Subdivided Networks]] also need not be a command hierarchy.
