# Grammaticalization

The historical development of grammatical functions from lexical expressions and the tighter organisation of previously looser linguistic sequences. In the usage-based account, repetition encourages chunking and phonetic reduction, while recurrent contextual inferences can become part of a construction's conventional meaning.

[[Language Is a Complex Adaptive System - Position Paper]] discusses the process on pp.7–9 ([source PDF](https://www.unm.edu/~jbybee/downloads/BecknerEtAl2009ComplexAdaptiveSystem.pdf#page=7)), using the development of *be going to* from movement toward a purpose into a future/intention expression as an example. It links this process to [[Usage-Based Grammar]] and cultural replication through utterances.

For artificial agents, conventionalising a repeated exchange into a reusable communication construct is a possible analogy. Installing a macro or shortening messages alone does not demonstrate the historical and semantic process described in human language.
