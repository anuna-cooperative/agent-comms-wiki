# Internal Knowledge Consistency

**Notation:** `consistency from every participant’s observable history`.

An interpretation may treat an idealisation as knowledge even when it is not literally true throughout the real system. It is internally knowledge consistent if a subsystem exists where that interpretation is factive and every participant’s local history in the full system occurs somewhere in that subsystem. Thus participants cannot observe a history refuting the idealisation. This explains certain safe uses of synchronous abstractions.
