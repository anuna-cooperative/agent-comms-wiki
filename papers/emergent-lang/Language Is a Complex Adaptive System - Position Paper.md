# Language Is a Complex Adaptive System: Position Paper

**Reference:** Clay Beckner, Richard Blythe, Joan Bybee, Morten H. Christiansen, William Croft, Nick C. Ellis, John Holland, Jinyun Ke, Diane Larsen-Freeman, and Tom Schoenemann (the “Five Graces Group”) (2009). *Language Learning*, 59(Supplement 1), 1–26. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2009.00533.x). [Author-hosted PDF](https://www.unm.edu/~jbybee/downloads/BecknerEtAl2009ComplexAdaptiveSystem.pdf). Printed and one-based PDF page numbers coincide.

## Summary

The authors argue that human language is a complex adaptive system: speakers interact, their histories of interaction shape subsequent behavior, competing cognitive and social pressures influence their choices, and linguistic structure emerges from those processes. Language acquisition, use, and change are interdependent aspects of one system rather than separate processes operating on a fixed grammar. This is a position paper synthesising linguistic, psychological, historical, and modelling research, not a new benchmark or a single experimental result.

The proposed [[Usage-Based Grammar]] consists of constructions—pairings of form and meaning—learned and reorganised through experience. Repetition changes accessibility, encourages chunking, and can turn frequently inferred meanings into conventional meanings. [[Grammaticalization]] describes how lexical expressions and loosely connected sequences develop grammatical functions and tighter organisation. Individual speakers' grammars remain open to change throughout life, while communal conventions emerge from interactions among speakers with different exposure histories.

For this wiki, the important connection is that communication can change the language used for subsequent communication. The paper explicitly treats speakers as interacting agents, but its subject is human language. Applying its account to artificial agents or CBCL is a proposed research direction; it does not establish that runtime dialect extension automatically produces adaptation, shared understanding, or improved coordination.

## Key Ideas

- **Language coordinates joint action (pp.3–5).** Communication is itself a joint action requiring participants to recognise communicative intentions. [[Convention (Lewis)|Conventions]] solve recurring coordination problems, but different usage histories and interpretations leave communication fallible.
- **Experience shapes grammar (pp.5–7).** [[Usage-Based Grammar]] treats grammar as a network of categorised instances of use. Constructions range from particular words and idioms to abstract patterns; lexical knowledge and grammar are intertwined.
- **Repeated use changes structure (pp.7–9).** [[Grammaticalization]] links repetition, phonetic reduction, contextual inference, and changes of meaning. The paper's example is the development of *be going to* from movement toward a purpose into a future/intention construction, including reduced *gonna*.
- **Cultural replication differs from organism reproduction (pp.9, 14).** Sounds, words, and constructions are reproduced in utterances, with variation and differential reuse. Change is not restricted to transmission between biological generations: speakers continue adapting throughout their lifetimes.
- **Learning and use form a feedback loop (pp.9–12).** Prior learning shapes perception of new input; perception shapes what is learned; learning changes subsequent usage. Adaptation can preserve entrenched patterns or simplify forms, rather than always increasing complexity.
- **Individual and collective emergence differ (pp.14–15).** An individual's idiolect emerges through participation in communal language; the community's language emerges from interacting idiolects. There is no ideal representative speaker whose knowledge substitutes for the heterogeneous population.
- **Competing pressures and network structure matter (pp.16–17).** Production economy can conflict with listener clarity; feedback can amplify small changes. Who interacts with whom affects the spread and establishment of variants.
- **Modelling requires independent checks (pp.12–14).** Reproducing an observed pattern does not prove the assumed mechanism. The authors call for independently supported processes, testable predictions, and empirical evidence about interaction networks.

## Connections

- [[Dynamics of Complex Systems]] — a general foundation for interdependence and collective organisation; this paper directly applies a complex-adaptive-systems perspective to human language. This comparison does not imply the paper cites Bar-Yam.
- [[Convention - Lewis]] and [[Shared Cooperative Activity]] — explicitly cited foundations for recurring coordination problems and joint action.
- [[Emergent Communication]] and [[Language Games for Autonomous Robots]] — situated interaction and feedback can establish shared conventions; the position paper situates such mechanisms within a broader account of human learning and language change.
- [[Emergent Semantics Principles and Issues]] — related bottom-up account of semantic agreement; heterogeneous individual histories make shared meaning a continuing achievement.
- [[Towards Automating the Evolution of Linguistic Competence]] and [[Toward Automated Evolution of ACLs]] — artificial-agent counterparts in which vocabulary changes are explicitly negotiated. Deliberate negotiation differs from the local, often unplanned changes emphasised here.
- [[Recipient-Relative Information]] and [[Common Ground]] — participants bring different prior experiences to interpretation; convention alone does not guarantee identical understandings.
- [[CBCL - Safe Self-Extending Agent Communication]] — **proposed application:** dialect extension could express new distinctions or conventionalised interaction patterns arising through use. Structural validation establishes admissibility under the relevant constraints; usefulness, adoption, and coordination benefits need separate evaluation. This paper does not study CBCL or establish the safety of evolved protocols.

## Conceptual Contribution

- **Claim:** Language structure emerges and changes through interacting speakers, usage histories, general cognitive processes, and social pressures; acquisition, use, and change belong to one complex adaptive system.
- **Mechanism:** Categorisation of experienced constructions, frequency-sensitive learning, chunking, contextual inference, differential replication of variants, and local interactions constrained by social networks.
- **Concepts introduced/used:** [[Usage-Based Grammar]], [[Grammaticalization]], [[Convention (Lewis)]], [[Emergent Communication]], [[Self-Organisation]], [[Coordination]], [[Multi-Agent Systems]]. These are concepts developed or used in the synthesis, not claims that the paper originated them.
- **Stance:** theoretical / usage-based linguistics / research position statement.
- **Relates to:** Connects the general complex-systems perspective of [[Dynamics of Complex Systems]] with language emergence and the artificial-agent language-evolution work in [[Towards Automating the Evolution of Linguistic Competence]]. It motivates studying changing communication languages without proving that every change is an improvement or that spontaneous change satisfies formal protocol constraints.
