# The Process and Effects of Mass Communication

**Reference:** Wilbur Schramm (ed.) (1954). *The Process and Effects of Mass Communication*. Urbana: University of Illinois Press. Linked scan: fourth printing, 1960. [Full-book PDF](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/BOOKSHELF-ARH/Education/The-Process-and-Effects-of-Mass-Communication-Schramm-1960.pdf).

## Summary

This edited collection brings together psychological and sociological accounts of communication. Schramm's opening chapter, “How Communication Works,” treats communication as establishing shared understanding through encoding, interpretation, and feedback.

The collection follows communication from attention and audience motivations through perception, attitude change, and group influence. Its international-communication and propaganda examples reflect its origin as background material for United States Information Agency training, rather than a single unified experimental study.

## Key Ideas

- Shared experience and cultural context shape interpretation.
- Feedback lets communicators adjust messages; mass communication complicates that return path.
- Attention and audience motivations mediate exposure to messages.
- Source credibility and existing attitudes affect persuasion.
- Group membership and personal influence mediate mass-media effects.
