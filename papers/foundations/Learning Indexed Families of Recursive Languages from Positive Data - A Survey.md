# Learning Indexed Families of Recursive Languages from Positive Data: A Survey

**Reference:** Steffen Lange, Thomas Zeugmann & Sandra Zilles (2008). *Theoretical Computer Science* 397(1–3), 194–232. [Publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304397508001503) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2008.02.030) · [Author-hosted preprint](https://www2.cs.uregina.ca/~zilles/langeZZ08survey.pdf).

## Summary

The survey studies [[Identification in the Limit]] for indexed families of recursive languages: membership is decidable uniformly from a language index and a string. Positive examples eventually enumerate the target; successful hypotheses stabilize on a correct description without necessarily announcing success.

It compares learning constraints, hypothesis spaces, query models, and efficiency. Decidable membership alone does not establish learnability from positive examples.

## Key Ideas

- Hypothesis representation affects learning power.
- Conservative, monotonic, and incremental learning impose distinct constraints.
- Identification and computational efficiency are separate requirements.
